# Changelog

Development milestones for the Deposplit project. Pre-release — entries are grouped by feature area rather than version number.

## Implemented

- **SSS libraries**: both the Kotlin (`Android/`) and Swift (`iOS/`) ports of Shamir's Secret Sharing are complete and fully tested.
- **Design**: the full app layer is designed — Web app/service protocol (4 message types + consent model), share holder onboarding, contact verification, identity recovery, contacts management, secret input methods, Ports & Adapters architecture. All documented in `CLAUDE.md`.
- **Android registration**: hexagonal boundary fully enforced for auth. `:hexagon/driving_ports/Identity` is the driving port, `:hexagon/driven_ports/IdentityStore` is the driven port, and `IdentityService` in `:hexagon/services/` owns all BouncyCastle crypto computation (Ed25519 + X25519 keypair generation, Ed25519 signing, X25519+HKDF-SHA-256+ChaCha20-Poly1305 encrypt/decrypt). `:app/auth/AndroidIdentityStore` implements `IdentityStore` using Android Keystore AES-256-GCM wrapping of private keys and `SharedPreferences` for storage; pseudonym and public keys stored plaintext. `DeposplitApp` wires `IdentityService(AndroidIdentityStore(this))` typed as `Identity`. All screens implemented: registration (`SignInView`), home with three tabs (Distributed/Held/Requests), contacts management with QR scan (`DataScannerViewController`) and manual entry, deposit flow (`Shamir.split` + `auth.encrypt` + `transport.depositShare`), recipient consent flows (approve/deny), sender-side reconstruction (`auth.decrypt` + `Shamir.combine`), QR display (CoreImage). Crypto via CryptoKit: X25519 key agreement + `hkdfDerivedSymmetricKey(using: SHA256.self, ...)` + `ChaChaPoly`. Ed25519 signing via `Curve25519.Signing.PrivateKey`. - **Holder-decrypts-at-pickup share-crypto redesign** (`deposplit.com/CLAUDE.md` "What is next" item 7): Replaced the encrypt-to-recipient *blind-courier* model — where a share stayed ciphertext bound to the sender's deposit-time keypair for its entire life, and so could never be reconstructed after the sender lost that keypair — with one where the holder decrypts at pickup and re-encrypts fresh at retrieve. **Pickup**: the holder now decrypts the deposited ciphertext with their own X25519 private key + the sender's X25519 public key (from their local contact record) and stores the resulting **plaintext** share — a single holder's `< k` share is information-theoretically empty on its own, so plaintext-at-rest (behind the OS's file-based encryption) is safe. **Retrieve**: the holder re-encrypts that stored plaintext to the *requester's current* X25519 public key, looked up live rather than pinned at deposit time — this is what makes k-of-n social recovery cryptographically possible at all, since no step depends on a keypair that might since have rotated or been lost. `reconstruct()` needed no code change on either platform, since its decrypt step already used the sender's own identity + the holder's *current* `xPublicKey`. `HeldShare.senderKey`/`ciphertext` → `contactId`/`senderPseudonym`/`plaintextShare`; `ShareMetadata.recipientKey` → `contactId` (both now a stable local UUID rather than a raw Ed25519 key, so the record survives a counterparty's key rotation); `ContactRepository` gained `getById` on all three hexagons. The **deposplit.com relay is entirely unchanged** — it still stores and forwards opaque ciphertext at both deposit and retrieve, blind at every phase; this is a client-only change to `hexagon`'s `ShareService`/`ShareRepository`/`ShareMetadataRepository` plus their local-storage adapters (`LocalShareRepository`/`LocalShareMetadataRepository` on Android and iOS). Applied to Android `:hexagon`/`:app`, iOS `hexagon`/`Deposplit`, and (for cross-platform *consistency*, not full parity — no denormalized pseudonym snapshot, since its held-shares view doesn't render a sender name yet) `deposplit.com/hexagons/phon` plus its `FileShareRepository`/`FileContactRepository`/`PhonyPhoneController`/`mySecrets.scala.html`. - **`ShareRequestType` → `ShareTransactionType` rename + wire-representation cleanup** (flagged during item 8, naming settled in a follow-up design discussion; see `deposplit.com/TODO.md`'s cross-cutting chores): The four request/transaction types read inconsistently — three verbs mixing command and announcement voice (`pick_up`, `retrieve`, `delete`) plus a bare noun (`recovery_metadata`) — and a "rename to verbs from Alice's point of view" first pass broke down once item 8's `recovery_metadata` was examined: unlike the other three (always opened by the sender), it's opened by the *holder*, so no single named actor's viewpoint stays accurate across all four. The resolution was to name the *transaction* itself, neutral of actor, borrowing a safe-deposit-box's vocabulary: `pick_up` → **`deposit`**, `retrieve` → **`retrieval`**, `delete` → **`removal`** (not `deletion` — stays in the same vault register as the other three instead of switching to database vocabulary for one case), `recovery_metadata` → **`inventory`** (a bank reporting "here's what's in your box" without handing over the contents — avoids implying the whole secret is "recovered," and avoids `metadata` as a name that means too many other things elsewhere in a software app). The type itself was renamed `ShareRequestType` → `ShareTransactionType` — not every case is consent-gated (`inventory` is a self-approving push), so "Transaction" is the honest umbrella term; "Share," not "Secret," since every row is scoped to one share held by one holder, never the secret as a whole. Scope was deliberately narrow: only the type, its four cases, and the `requestType` → `transactionType` field/property rename — `ShareRequest`, the `share_requests` DB table, `ShareRequestsController`/`ShareRequestsService`, and the `Shares`/`ShareRequests` port were left unchanged, since a row is still meaningfully "a request" for three of the four transaction kinds. The pass also converged the wire-representation architecture across platforms, which had drifted: Swift already baked its wire string into the enum's own `rawValue` (one source of truth); Scala and Kotlin kept their enums bare but then hand-duplicated the case→string mapping independently in several adapters each (a real risk of missing one on the next new case, as had almost happened before). Scala and Kotlin were converged *toward* Swift's shape rather than the reverse — both now carry a `wireValue` per case plus a `fromWire` companion directly on the enum, since `PayloadCanonical` (domain, on every platform) already needs the wire string for signing regardless, so this isn't actually a hexagonal-purity violation, just consolidation. Every hand-rolled `toWire`/`requestTypeStr` match was deleted in favour of `.wireValue`/`.fromWire`. `ShareRepository.hasActivePickUp` → `hasActiveDeposit` (relay), and several client-side mechanism-level names that directly embedded the old case names were renamed alongside their call sites: `HolderStatus.retrieveRequest` → `.retrievalRequest` (Android/iOS), `ShareDetailViewModel`'s `retrieveRequest`/`deleteRequest`/`approvedRetrieveCount`/`isOpeningRetrieve`/`isOpeningDelete` → `retrievalRequest`/`removalRequest`/`approvedRetrievalCount`/`isOpeningRetrieval`/`isOpeningRemoval` and its `openRetrieveRequest()`/`openDeleteRequest()` → `openRetrievalRequest()`/`openRemovalRequest()` (Android), plus matching string-resource renames in both `values/strings.xml` and `values-de/strings.xml`. Deliberately **kept** `ShareManagement.pushRecoveryMetadata`/`ShareService.processRecoveryMetadata` and their test names unchanged on every client platform: those name the item-8 *feature* (identity recovery), not the wire transaction type, the same way a `refundPurchase()` method can be implemented via a generic `Transaction(type: .credit)` without needing its own name to track the generic type's — applied consistently, mechanism-level test names were renamed (e.g. Android/phon's `pickUpRow` → `depositRow`) while feature-level ones (`pushRecoveryMetadata opens...`, `approvedRecoveryMetadataRow`) were kept. The DB enum type `share_request_type` → `share_transaction_type` and its column `request_type` → `transaction_type` were renamed in place in `1.sql` (no evolution — pre-launch, no migrations policy). One Twirl view, `app/views/Phon/pendingRequests.scala.html`, rendered `@shareRequest.requestType` directly and needed fixing too — missed by an initial grep sweep scoped to `.scala` sources, a reminder to grep templates explicitly when a rendered field is renamed. Implemented in the established `R` (foundational — DB schema + wire protocol) → iOS → Android → phon order; the cross-platform `PayloadCanonical` signature vector was recomputed once (from an actual BouncyCastle run, not hand-derived) and the identical value propagated to all three client suites, confirming byte-identical canonical construction survived the rename. - **Malicious key substitution + stolen-key revocation** (`deposplit.com/CLAUDE.md` "What is next" item 10, shipped 2026-08-18): Closes the key-change thread item 9 opened. Load-bearing reframe: a stolen private key is not a stolen secret — an attacker must still defeat `k` holders' out-of-band retrieve-consent, so the k-of-n consent layer, not the keypair, is the real backstop. Since a compromised key can't cryptographically revoke itself (Alice and an attacker holding `K_stolen` are indistinguishable — both can sign), revocation is **socially anchored** instead: `Contact` gained `revokedEdKeys: [Data]`/`List<ByteArray>` — a growing historical *set*, not a single boolean, so a later legitimate relink to a genuinely new key is never blocked by an old flag — and `keyChangedAt`, surfaced as a "key changed N days ago" indicator specifically on retrieval-request approval (the attack signature is key-change-then-quick-retrieval). `ContactManagement.markKeyCompromised` (idempotent, defaults to the contact's current key) flags a key; `ShareService.processRotations()` (item 9) now checks an incoming rotation notice's `oldEd25519Key` against `revokedEdKeys` *before* its existing downgrade/auto-accept branch — on a match it captures a new local-only `KeyConflict` record (a new `KeyConflictRepository` driven port) *before* deleting the relay notice, since the relay may lose its state at any time and the alert must never depend on its retention, and skips `updateContact` entirely; conflicts are **never auto-resolved**, only dismissed once the user has independently relinked via the existing item-8 flow.
